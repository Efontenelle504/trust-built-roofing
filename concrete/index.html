A straightforward process

From first look to final wash-down.

Concrete work has many moving parts. The process keeps the scope, site, and schedule clear before the truck arrives.

  1. 01

    Site review

    We start with the project goal, measurements, photos, access, drainage, existing concrete, and nearby structures.

  2. 02

    Scope & estimate

    You receive a practical scope covering removal, preparation, placement, finish, cleanup, and known assumptions.

  3. 03

    Prep & placement

    Forms, subbase, reinforcement, elevations, and access are checked before concrete placement and finishing.

  4. 04

    Finish & cure

    We review the finished surface, cleanup, initial cure guidance, and when normal foot or vehicle traffic can resume.

Concrete questions

Know what to ask before the pour.

A good estimate should explain more than square footage. These are a few of the questions that shape scope, timing, and long-term performance.

It depends on why it failed. Small isolated damage may be repairable, while widespread cracking, settlement, poor drainage, or a weak base often point toward partial or full replacement. The goal is to address the cause—not only the visible crack.

Concrete naturally shrinks as it cures and moves with temperature and ground conditions. Proper base preparation, reinforcement, mix placement, curing, and well-planned control joints help manage where movement occurs and reduce avoidable cracking.

Timing depends on the mix, weather, thickness, and project type. Foot traffic may be allowed sooner than vehicle traffic. Your project closeout should include clear guidance for the first days of curing and when normal use can resume.

Common factors include demolition and disposal, access, excavation, grading, subbase, thickness, reinforcement, total area, finish type, curves or steps, drainage changes, and the number of mobilizations required.

Yes. The right finish should be chosen around appearance, traction, exposure, cleaning, sealing, and maintenance. Decorative borders or selective stamped areas can create impact without adding complexity everywhere.
